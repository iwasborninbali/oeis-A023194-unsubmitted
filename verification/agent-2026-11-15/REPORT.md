## Proof check

Line-by-line audit of [`proof.md`](../../proof.md), distinguishing mathematical
hypotheses from deficiencies in presentation:

1. **Line 3, first sentence (multiplicativity). CORRECT, with a fixable
   omitted edge case.** If `sigma(n)` is prime and `n>1`, `n` cannot have two
   distinct prime divisors since their divisor sums are nonunit factors.
   `n=1` is silently omitted from the inference `n=p^e`; explicitly state
   `sigma(1)=1`, not prime. No change to the result.
2. **Line 3, remaining sentences (prime repunit length). CORRECT.** For
   `n=p^e`, `sigma(n)=1+p+...+p^e`. If `e+1=ab`, both displayed factors of
   the geometric series exceed one; hence `q=e+1` must be prime. The
   argument uses only elementary factorization.
3. **Line 5, first sentence (distinct bases). CORRECT.** The geometric sum
   is strictly increasing in exponent at fixed base. Every prime divisor
   sum has a prime base after step 1.
4. **Line 5, two-digit case. CORRECT, with a fixable explanation.** For
   prime `p>2`, `p+1` is even and greater than two. For `p=2`, the value is
   3, whereas every length-at-least-three repunit in a base at least 2 is
   at least 7. Include these inequalities to document the exclusion. It
   follows that any collision requires two *different prime* bases, each
   with length >=3.
5. **Line 7, first sentence. CONDITIONAL, NOT PROVED HERE.** The
   Goormaghtigh conjecture asserts that the only numbers possessing two
   length-at-least-three repunit representations with distinct integer
   bases are 31 and 8191. It remains unproved. This dependency is **fatal
   to an unconditional/global uniqueness claim**, and cannot be repaired
   by increasing any finite sieve. It is **not** a flaw in a theorem
   explicitly stated *assuming* the conjecture. To prove global
   uniqueness unconditionally one would need a new theorem excluding
   two prime-base prime-valued repunits, possibly weaker than the full
   Goormaghtigh conjecture.
6. **Line 7, 31. CORRECT.** `31=1+2+4+8+16=1+5+25` gives
   `sigma(16)=sigma(25)=31`. Exhaustive bounds are justified by
   `1+b+b^2 <= 31`, so `b<=5`, and `2^L-1 <=31`, so `L<=5`.
   Our separate program recovers precisely `(base,length)=(2,5),(5,3)`.
7. **Line 7, 8191. CORRECT.** For length >=3, `1+b+b^2 <=8191` gives
   `b<=90`; the minimum length-`L` repunit is `2^L-1`, giving `L<=13`.
   Independent enumeration finds only `(2,13),(90,3)`; 90 is not prime.
   Accordingly, `sigma(4096)=8191` has no second prime-power preimage.
   All 31/8191 primality checks and the asserted preimages were computed
   directly again, including direct divisor pairing on the witness `n`.
8. **Line 7, final conclusion. CORRECT ONLY CONDITIONALLY.** A finite
   enumeration of the two *conjecturally* exhaustive values cannot prove
   no additional collision exists elsewhere. The proof explicitly says
   this; an unconditional interpretation would be unjustified.

**Proof verdict: CORRECT as a conditional proof**, with the two minor,
fixable explanatory omissions in steps 1 and 4. The conjectural dependency in
step 5 is **fatal** to an unconditional reading. No incorrect equality or
computational assertion was found. A separate provenance limitation is that
`CERTIFICATE.md` records the guide's independent check as `pending` and does
not independently audit Grantham's external proof/code; the latter is expressly
outside its declared coverage.

## Independent computation

New program: [`independent_check.py`](independent_check.py), with its complete
stdout in [`output.log`](output.log). Both were written for this audit without
importing or copying the repository's sieve/checker or an OEIS term table.
The algorithm instead enumerates the **necessary prime powers**
`n = p^(q-1)`, where `p,q` are primes. The `q=2` case contributes only `n=2`;
for every odd prime `q`, it evaluates the repunit formula
`(p^q-1)/(p-1)` for all candidate prime bases with `n<=10^14`. Primes `p`
are generated by an Eratosthenes bytearray sieve. Prime-valued repunits are
identified using the seven-base deterministic Miller–Rabin test for integers
below `2^64`. All tested divisor sums are below `2*10^14`, hence within that
integer range. Direct trial-divisor pairing separately checks the small
witnesses and all positive terms up to 100000. A second, distinct geometric
repunit-growth search enumerates all bases and lengths for 31 and 8191.

* **Range:** EVERY integer argument `1 <= n <= 100000000000000` is covered
  by the proven prime-power reduction; this is a bound on **arguments**,
  not on the common prime or on every Goormaghtigh number.
* **Counts:** 664579 prime bases through `10^7`, 665104 candidate prime powers,
  33733 arguments with prime sigma and 33732 different prime sigma values.
* **Collisions:** **exactly `[(31, [16, 25])]`** in this range.
* **Original certificate's range rechecked:** through `100000`, exactly
  23 prime-sigma arguments, with the same collision; all 23 terms appear
  in `output.log` and match OEIS A023194's published prefix.
* **Witnesses:** 31 only `(base,length)=(2,5),(5,3)`;
  8191 only `(2,13),(90,3)`; only `(2,13)` uses a prime base.
  Direct sums: `sigma(16)=31`, `sigma(25)=31`, `sigma(4096)=8191`.
* **Runtime:** Python program's wall clock `3.339` seconds; external
  `/usr/bin/time` reported `4.52` elapsed seconds, `4.44` user CPU
  seconds, `0.42` system CPU seconds, maximum RSS 132760 KiB on this
  container. All assertions passed.
* **Program SHA-256:**
  `807ebc6760fcf5febd4ed8287691feddfacbcfa3ce933699d6aed3b140aff724`.
* **Output-log SHA-256:**
  `9c70161810506c971c2b2570bbae1058332021ea79074e15872e8c163f6f2d57`.

The locally reproduced observations do not establish the missing infinite
case or independently replicate Grantham's `10^700` result.

## Verdict

**COMPLETE for the certificate's exact, limited meta-claim and the
computational parts it actually claims.** The conditional proof is valid,
the 1..100000 computation and 31/8191 calculations were reproduced
independently, and an entirely different computation found no further
collisions throughout arguments 1..10^14. The quoted OEIS and Grantham
texts confirm that global uniqueness depends on an unproved conjecture.

**NOT ESTABLISHED** for the stronger, *unconditional* statement "31 is the
only prime equal to `sigma(n)` for two distinct positive integers", or any
assertion of novelty. Grantham's full proof and `10^700` computation have not
been independently certified in this audit. This boundary is essential: the
certificate's own `complete` label does not license the stronger result.
